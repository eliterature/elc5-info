We have now completed our Round 1 and Round 2 phases of reviewing and are preparing to select between 50 to 75 entries for the collection based on scoring from external reviewers and internal discussions. This post explains these processes and our next steps.

## Round 1

As discussed in our May 2026 update, we previously completed our Round 0 of reviewing. This consisted of checking for duplicates, verifying all submitted materials could be accessed, and making notes about works potentially needing special attention. From this step, we compiled a list of reviewers and assigned them works based on language skills or special expertise.

As part of this reviewing process, we asked reviewers to consider the following criteria:

* **Legibility as e-lit**:  We consider eligible literary works as any work fundamentally shaped by its digital nature, whether they exist purely on screens, in hybrid formats, in print, or in physical spaces.
* **Criticality, intentionality, reflexivity, and specificity of medium**: We are especially interested in works operating with critical awareness of the platforms and systems they inhabit. As distinctions between electronic literature and other digital art forms continue to blur, we prioritize works foregrounding language, narrative, and meaning-making, even as they move across genres and media.
* **Supporting e-lit pedagogy**: We want to recognize works with scholarly, pedagogical, and archival value, including those produced with or from critical/scholarly tools.

Reviewers were asked rate a work on a Likert scale of 1 (strongly recommend) to 5 (Strongly Do Not Recommend) on if a work matched at least two of the criteria and provide a rationale for the choice.

Based on these results, we then internally verified all works had a reviewer score and helped any reviewers who faced issues with accessing a work.

## Round 2

Using Round 1 scores, we filtered the collection into those works with higher and lower scores. We then internally began our Round 2 deliberations.

Round 2 was a three-step process. First, we reviewed those works with the lowest scores to confirm if these works would continue into the next round of reviewing. Second, we performed a simple "Yes/No" review pass of the remaining works along with internal notes for discussion. Any works with four "Yes" scores were set aside and discussions focused on disagreements on works with mixed scoring.
